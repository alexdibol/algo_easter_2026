# Algorithmic Trading Systems Easter Course  
## Cambridge Judge Business School · University of Cambridge

This repository hosts the landing page for the **Algorithmic Trading Systems Easter Course**, a governance-first course architecture designed for Master of Finance students and financial professionals who need to understand algorithmic trading as a complete institutional system.

The landing page provides direct access to the core course materials and explains how students can navigate the broader learning ecosystem before class, during class, and after class.

---

## Course Purpose

The course presents algorithmic trading not merely as a collection of strategies, signals, indicators, or backtests, but as a disciplined system of design, implementation, testing, execution, auditability, and institutional governance.

The central idea of the course is that an algorithmic trading system must be understood as a complete operating structure. It connects:

- data;
- signal design;
- model logic;
- portfolio construction;
- execution;
- transaction costs;
- slippage;
- stress testing;
- risk management;
- logs and audit trails;
- final reporting;
- human responsibility.

In this sense, the course treats algorithmic trading as a professional system under governance, not as a collection of disconnected coding exercises.

## Course Methodology

The course is built around a multi-format learning architecture.

The books and decks are not isolated files. They operate as structured gateways into the full course ecosystem. Chapter by chapter, they guide students toward the relevant:

1. books and papers;
2. videos;
3. podcasts;
4. specialized decks;
5. Google Colab notebooks.

This allows students to dive into the material before and after class through interactive resources that match their curiosity, pace, and learning intentions.

One student may begin with the book and then move into notebooks. Another may begin with a deck, watch the video, listen to the podcast, and then return to the chapter. Another may use the materials to explore a specific trading idea, agentic workflow, or governance problem.

The course is therefore designed not only to be completed, but to be revisited.

---

## Governance-First Framework

The course is organized around the principle that algorithmic trading systems must be governed from the beginning.

Governance is not treated as a final compliance appendix. It is part of the system itself.

Key governance concepts include:

- clear system design;
- data provenance;
- signal explainability;
- model assumptions;
- execution constraints;
- transaction costs;
- slippage;
- stress testing;
- risk controls;
- benchmark discipline;
- audit logs;
- reproducibility;
- failure analysis;
- final reporting;
- human oversight;
- responsible use of AI.

An algorithmic system that cannot be explained, reproduced, audited, stress-tested, and reviewed is not yet a professional system.

## Course Architecture

The Easter course is organized around four major resource modules.

### Module 1. Course Presentation

This module introduces the course, its objectives, its learning architecture, and the governance-first framing of algorithmic trading systems.

### Module 2. Algo Trading Playground

This module supports creativity, experimentation, and early-stage strategy exploration. It allows students to test hypotheses, compare strategy families, and use AI responsibly as a research assistant.

### Module 3. Algo Trading Agents

This module introduces the use of agents in algorithmic trading workflows. It explores how AI can support research, coding, evaluation, risk review, and reporting while keeping human accountability at the center.

### Module 4. Algo Trading Lab

This module focuses on implementation. It brings together notebooks, experiments, backtests, stress tests, audit artifacts, and governed templates.

## What Students Should Be Able to Do

By working through the course materials, students should be able to:

1. explain algorithmic trading as a complete system rather than a single trading rule;
2. distinguish strategy intuition from governed implementation;
3. understand the role of data, signals, execution, risk, and reporting in a trading workflow;
4. evaluate trading ideas using structured backtesting discipline;
5. understand the importance of transaction costs and slippage;
6. design stress tests for trading systems;
7. use AI responsibly in trading research and documentation;
8. understand how agentic workflows can support algorithmic trading research;
9. produce more transparent and reviewable trading artifacts;
10. distinguish experimental notebooks from production systems;
11. explain why human responsibility remains central in algorithmic trading;
12. connect creativity, implementation, and governance into one coherent workflow.

---

## Use of AI in the Course

The course does not discourage the use of AI.

On the contrary, AI can be a valuable tool for research, explanation, code assistance, documentation, debugging, and creative exploration. However, AI use must be transparent, controlled, and accountable.

Students should be able to explain:

- where AI was used;
- what tasks AI supported;
- what human review was performed;
- what assumptions were checked;
- what outputs were accepted, revised, or rejected;
- how the final work reflects human responsibility.

AI use should not replace understanding. It should support deeper inquiry, better documentation, and more disciplined experimentation.

---

## Educational Use and Non-Reliance

All trading strategies, notebooks, backtests, simulations, examples, workflows, and AI outputs associated with this course are educational artifacts.

They are not investment advice, trading recommendations, portfolio management services, financial promotions, or production-ready trading systems.

Any real-world use requires independent verification, professional review, appropriate risk controls, and compliance with applicable laws, regulations, institutional policies, and platform terms of service.

Students should treat all course outputs as learning instruments, not as trading instructions.
